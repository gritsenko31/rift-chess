    How to play Rift Chess

    Rift Chess is ordinary chess with one extra mechanic: two permanent pairs of portals.

    • Pair A: c3 ↔ f6 (cyan diamond)
    • Pair B: f3 ↔ c6 (violet circle)

    Portals

    1. Make an ordinary chess move with any piece except the king.
    2. If you capture on the landing square, the capture happens normally.
    3. If the piece lands on a portal and the twin square is empty, it must teleport there, as part of the same move.
    4. If the twin square holds any piece, friend or foe, there is no teleport and the piece stays where it landed. Nothing is ever captured at the exit.
    5. A piece teleports at most once per move and never bounces back. Sliding over a portal, or already standing on one, does nothing.
    6. Kings never teleport. They may stand on portal squares, and a king on an exit blocks that portal.

    Pieces attack only from where they stand, using normal chess geometry. A portal never lets a piece attack or capture at a distance. Pawns keep moving toward the opponent's side after a teleport.

    Both portals of a pair have the same square colour, so bishops stay on their colour.

    Note: when a bishop or queen on one portal moves along the diagonal to its twin, it leaves its own square empty. It then teleports straight back to where it started, capturing anything on the far portal along the way.

    Check and legality

    A move is legal if your king is safe after the whole move, including captures, promotion and teleport. Checkmate, stalemate, castling, en passant and promotion (queen, rook, bishop or knight) work as in standard chess.

    Draws

    • Stalemate, fivefold repetition, the 75-move rule and recognised dead positions end the game automatically.
    • Threefold repetition and the fifty-move rule can be claimed with the Claim Draw button. That button appears only when a claim is valid, either now or by playing a specific move.
    • In two-player mode you can offer a draw, and your opponent can accept or decline it.

    Controls

    Tap or click a piece, then a highlighted square. Dots mark quiet moves and rings mark captures. A portal badge shows where the piece will come out, or that the exit is blocked. On a keyboard, arrow keys move between squares and Enter or Space selects.

    Move notation

    The move list uses a custom coordinate notation, not standard algebraic notation. For example: Nb1-c3→f6 (knight moved to portal c3 and came out on f6), e5xd6 e.p., e7-e8=Q, O-O. A trailing + means check and # means checkmate.
